While deep learning sequence-to-function models are increasingly used to predict regulatory variant effects, systematic cross-model benchmarking against experimental ground truth from Massively Parallel Reporter Assays (MPRAs) across diverse cellular contexts remains limited.

We present a scalable benchmarking pipeline evaluating three sequence-to-function (seq2func) models — Enformer, Basenji2, and AlphaGenome — against MPRA-measured allelic regulatory activity across three cellular contexts: HepG2 (147,485 SNVs), HEK293T (147,532 SNVs), and NGN2-differentiated neurons (38,968 SNVs). Per-variant SAD scores were aggregated across all model output tracks and cell-type-matched subsets. Performance was assessed by Spearman correlation, AUROC, and AUPRC on all variants and the statistically significant subset (α = 0.1). Foundation models (HyenaDNA, DNABERT-2) are included for comparison.

AlphaGenome consistently outperformed the other models. On significant variants, it achieved Spearman ρ = 0.65 in HepG2 and ρ = 0.60 in HEK293T, compared to Basenji2 (ρ = 0.56 / 0.53) and Enformer (ρ = 0.55 / 0.53). In NGN2-differentiated neurons, all models performed markedly worse (Enformer ρ = 0.17, Basenji2 ρ = 0.19, AlphaGenome ρ = 0.16), with no clear winner, consistent with underrepresentation of neuronal contexts in seq2func training data. Cell-type-specific track filtering improved performance for Basenji2 and AlphaGenome but degraded Enformer outside liver contexts, revealing differential sensitivity to track selection. These results highlight the advantage of AlphaGenome in well-represented cellular contexts and suggest training data composition as a key determinant of cross-context generalization. This pipeline provides a reproducible framework for benchmarking regulatory variant predictors and will be extended to additional model classes and genomic contexts.
